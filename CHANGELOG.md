# Doctor Who Mod — Changelog

Fork changes on top of DrGmes' Doctor Who Mod (Architectury multi-loader mod: `common` / `fabric` / `neoforge`, package `net.drgmes.dwm`, Minecraft 1.21.1). Newest first.

Build: there is no Gradle wrapper. Use Gradle 8.14 with JDK 21, e.g. `gradle build`. Datagen is `gradle :fabric:runDatagen`; its output (`common/src/main/generated`) is tracked in git, so re-run it and commit the result whenever recipes, blocks or worldgen builders change.

## 1.0.35 — Silent Travel and Emergency Return

- **Silent Travel lever** (`SILENT_TRAVEL`), next to Landing Shields (`control_lever_4`, hotspot `(-0.725, 0.605, 0.0875)`): with it on, the TARDIS travels without a sound. Nothing plays for a demat or remat inside or outside (including the interior takeoff, landing and in-flight loop sounds), and a flyover makes none either: no takeoff sound, no fly-by sound, no early exterior thud. The exterior light no longer comes back on by itself when it lands (it still goes off for takeoff), so a lit lamp doesn't give it away; the lever turns it on. The exterior doors closing for takeoff make no sound either. Interior sounds that aren't travel (lever flips, shields, doors) are unaffected. The one exception is the interior thud, played when a flight ends whether it was a flyover or an ordinary landing. Flippable any time, saved with the TARDIS. It is linked to the Dematerialization Circuit: without one installed the lever won't flip ("Dematerialization Circuit Not Installed") and the setting counts as off, coming back as it was left if the circuit is put back. Manual demat/remat from the console is silent too, but plays no thud (it isn't a flight). New in English, Russian and Ukrainian (the last two unchecked by a native speaker).
- **Emergency Return lever** (`EMERGENCY_RETURN`), the last lever of that row (`control_lever_5`, hotspot `(-0.725, 0.605, 0.175)`): with it on, the TARDIS looks after its owner's last moments. When the owner drops to half a heart it tolls the cloister bell (`tardis_cloister_bell`, inside and, while the TARDIS is materialized, at its exterior too) twice; when they die it tolls three times and then takes itself to a spot within 20 blocks of the bed they would respawn at, or of the world spawn if they have none (or the bed is gone). The tolls are 3.25 seconds apart (`EMERGENCY_CHIME_INTERVAL` in `DWM.TIMINGS`), and that is the length of the sound: one toll cut from a recording of the bell whose tolls were 4.75 seconds apart (a second less, and then half a second less again), and made about 3 dB louder with a soft limiter, as the game can't play a sound louder than its file. It is mono, so it is heard from where it is, and it fades out cleanly where the old `tardis_bell` is cut off; that one is left for the "not yours" refusal. The trip ignores the handbrake but needs fuel or energy and the flight and materialization systems, like the starter, and it obeys Silent Travel and the flyover lever. It picks the safe spot closest to the bed's floor from sixteen random tries, whatever the vertical scanning mode. Nothing happens for travel if the TARDIS is already within 20 blocks of that spot; a flight already under way is left alone. The bed is noted when they die, so respawning early doesn't cancel it. Saved with the TARDIS. It is linked to the Dematerialization Circuit and the Directional Unit both: without either the lever won't flip and it counts as off. It is armed for one trip only: every landing after a flight, its own included, turns it off again, so it has to be thrown again for the next trip; a flight that lands after the owner has died therefore ends any pending return. New in English, Russian and Ukrainian (the last two unchecked by a native speaker).
- **The flight sound as the flyover comes in and goes out**: the flight sound (`tardis_flight`, the one a fly-by loops) now plays for the other flyovers too, and follows them and gets quieter with distance like the fly-by's (`FLIGHT_SOUND_VOLUME` and `FLIGHT_SOUND_RANGE` in `DWM.FLYOVER`, formerly `FLYBY_SOUND_*`), so it is only heard from close by. A short hop, which is in sight the whole way, loops it for as long as it moves sideways, like a fly-by: from when it gets going after the lift-off or fade-in until it has all but stopped over its landing spot, ahead of the plunge or fade-out (it counts as stopped when it moves slower than `WHOOSH_STOP_SPEED`, 0.1 blocks a tick, since it creeps in at each end), fading over the last second. The rest make one whoosh, timed to end as they slam down or vanish: by the time a long hop's arrival has stopped over its landing spot, at the touchdown of a drop from another dimension, and at the moment a long hop's departure streak disappears. It is only heard while the flyover moves sideways: not while it fades in at flying height after an underground takeoff, not during the lift-off, and not at all for the straight-up ascent of an interdimensional departure (a drop, which falls, is the exception). If the moving part is shorter than the sound, it is cut short and faded at its end. Silent Travel mutes all of it. A flyover now changes its sound as it goes (the takeoff's, then this), and a sound reads how long it lasts on its first tick, so a change can't cut the earlier one short. New sound value `FLIGHT_ONCE`; a server and its players need the same build, as with the rest of the flyover.
- **Slam landings at unloaded spots**: a short hop to an open-sky spot nobody was near (its chunk not loaded) landed with a normal materialization instead of the slam. Its landing is planned as it sets off, and that plan looked without loading the chunk, so an unloaded spot read as "covered" and the answer was kept for the landing. The plan now loads the destination chunk to look, as the landing does anyway (only short hops, at most 256 blocks away, plan this way; a long hop's arrival is only planned once its chunk is loaded, and its landing looks again with the chunk loaded).

## 1.0.30 — TARDIS flyover

A cosmetic flying TARDIS that plays during flights, toggled by a lever on the console. (From 1.0.35 the lever is linked to the Directional Unit: without one installed it won't flip and the flyover is off, coming back as it was left if the unit is put back.) The lever is `control_lever_2`, next to the outer light lever, in both the Imperial and Toyota consoles. It is saved with the TARDIS, so a key summon uses it too. With the lever off, everything behaves exactly as in 1.0.20.

**Status:** played through on Fabric, in single player and on a dedicated server with other players: the lift-off, the landings, the fly-bys and the sounds all work as described. It compiles on NeoForge, which has not been run. Datagen output is unchanged. Every number below is a `FLYOVER_*` constant in `DWM.java` (`DWM.TIMINGS`) and is meant to be tuned by watching it.

### What it does

Each end of a flight is judged on its own. "Above ground" means open sky over the spot, ignoring leaves and water, so the sea counts as open (`WorldHelper.isOpenToSky`).

| | Departure | Arrival |
|---|---|---|
| Above ground | No demat fade. The exterior stays until its block is removed and the flying copy appears in that same tick. It then makes a heavy lift-off: it hardly moves at first (about a second to get a block clear, with a slight strain-shake), then climbs to flying height, and flies away. | The flyover flies in, plunges straight down and slams onto the landing spot at the exterior's facing. No remat fade: the exterior is simply there, with a thud and a puff of ground debris. |
| Below ground | Normal demat underground. The flyover then fades in at flying height above the spot, so it never visibly passes through terrain. | The flyover flies in, fades to nothing over the spot, then the normal remat happens underground. |

- **Short hops (256 blocks or less)** are one continuous flight. Flight time is `max(145, 85 + distance / 4)` ticks.
- **Long hops** get a departure streak (accelerates away toward the destination and vanishes, reaching 160 blocks out). If a player is within about 190 blocks of the destination and its chunks are loaded, a separate arrival flyover streaks in from 144 blocks out and finishes like the tail of a short hop. Its flight time is `60 + distance / 12` ticks - fast, because most of a long hop is space nobody is watching - plus 5 seconds of idle padding, so that flying with nobody around isn't a cheat code for instant travel: about 12 seconds for 1,000 blocks, 21 for 3,000, and 30 for 5,000.
- **The whole trip is capped at 45 seconds**, as the key summon's wait: demat + flight + padding + remat - or **60 seconds if the trip is both interdimensional and long distance** (further than a short hop, 256 blocks by the raw coordinates); a trip that is only one of the two keeps the 45. The flight's budget is the cap minus the demat and remat it will actually use (instant ones cost almost nothing; the normal fades leave less). Only with the lever on: without it, flights keep the mod's own timing. **Fly-bys are the only thing allowed past the cap**, and they aren't limited in number.
- **Fly-bys.** Anyone standing near the middle of a long flight gets a fly-by: a short pass across their sky, slowest right in front of them. Every half second the flight looks at the players in the world for anyone within 80 blocks of the line between takeoff and landing whom the TARDIS hasn't passed yet, and queues a fly-by for them. One fly-by covers every player near enough to see it. There is no cap on how many a flight can have.
- **The flight slows down for a fly-by, only there.** The flight has a virtual position - how far along its line it is - that reaches the destination by the time the last 100 ticks begin, which belong to the arrival flyover (so the arrival always ends when the TARDIS lands, whatever happened on the way). When the virtual position reaches the stretch around a bystander, the fly-by starts and the flight's countdown slows for just that stretch, so it is crossed in exactly the 120 ticks (6 seconds) the fly-by takes. Everywhere else the flight runs at its normal pace. The extra time is taken out of the padding first, so a flight that stops for fly-bys is only as long as one that doesn't until the padding is used up; after that each fly-by adds about 5 seconds, past the 45-second cap. A fly-by is always 20 ticks longer than an arrival flyover, so someone standing under the route sees it for at least as long as someone it flies in to land beside: measured within 128 blocks of the viewer, an arrival is visible for about 5.0 seconds, a departure for 5.2, and a fly-by for 5.5 to 6.0. The console's progress bar includes the padding.
- **A fly-by carries on into the streak and the arrival.** Its path is up to 120 blocks each side of the bystander, but stops where the departure streak's reach ends (160 blocks from takeoff) and where the arrival flyover begins (144 blocks from landing), so the TARDIS is seen to keep going into them instead of vanishing and reappearing. A player too close to an end for even a 30-block pass is covered by the streak or the arrival flyover, and the arrival flyover's spawn radius (192 blocks) is set to reach exactly those players, so every stretch of the route gets one or the other.
- **Slow where it can be seen, fast where it can't.** The departure streak starts slow and rockets off; the arrival comes in fast from out of sight and slows to a crawl near the landing spot; the fly-by is slowest in front of the bystander.
- **Lit up at night, with or without shaders.** The model is drawn with block light 15 - the same a landed TARDIS stands in, since its lit top block gives off the maximum - and its lamp at full brightness, so it can be seen against a dark sky. Block light is also what shader packs light entities with, so it doesn't depend on the lamp's glow layer, which is skipped under shaders (as it is for the landed exterior).
- **Interdimensional hops follow the same rule at both ends** as a landing does: take-off and landing are each instant with open sky overhead, and the normal fade otherwise.
  - *Leaving* with open sky overhead (the Overworld, the End, the Aether - anything without a ceiling): no demat fade, the flying copy takes over from the exterior on its spot, lifts off heavily and accelerates straight up through the clouds and out of sight (75 ticks, no spin - it is vertical). Underground, or in a dimension with a ceiling: the normal demat, and nothing else.
  - *Arriving* on an above-ground spot with open sky (needs a player within 128 blocks to see it): a flyover drops straight down from the sky and slams onto the spot. Underground, under a ceiling, or with nobody near: the normal remat, exactly as if the lever weren't pulled - it does not fly in from the distance or shoot up.
  - The flight itself has no route to fly, so it keeps the mod's own distance formula (plus its 15-second interdimensional bonus), within the cap above.
- **Ceilinged dimensions (`hasCeiling()`: the Nether and anything built like it) don't turn the lever off.** It stays switched on. Take-off and landing there are simply the normal fades, and nothing flies in or out of a place with no open sky - but a trip between a ceilinged dimension and an open one still uses the flyover at the open end (leaving the Overworld for the Nether flies straight up; leaving the Nether for the Overworld ends in the slam). A trip where *both* ends have a ceiling (a hop within the Nether) ignores the lever completely, timing included: the mod's own fades and flight time, with no cap.
- **Rotation only during horizontal flight.** The model spins at about 9° per tick while it moves horizontally and is at rest during a fade-in, slam, fade-out and drop. A flyover that ends over a target slows to a stop on the last stretch of its travel, exactly on the facing the exterior will have, before it hovers above the spot. The spin is planned up front, so the cruise rate flexes (about 5 to 12°) instead of the model ever speeding up to reach the facing.
- **Slam handoff timing.** The plunge finishes 3 ticks before the exterior appears and the entity lingers 2 ticks after, because clients see entities a few ticks late.

### Changes

- **Flyover entity** (`entities/tardis/exteriors/`): `TardisFlyoverEntity` is one timeline of a departure phase (a heavy lift-off, or a fade-in), a travel phase (route, departure streak, approach or fly-by) and a tail (slam or fade-out). The spin is synced as its own unbounded tracked value, not the entity's wrapped yaw, so it doesn't glitch on other clients. `TardisFlyoverEntityRenderer` poses the model exactly like `BaseTardisExteriorBlockRenderer` (same scale, offsets and rotation), with the doors drawn closed, so handoffs with the real exterior line up. The entity's render range is raised to 256 blocks (vanilla would cull it at about 96).
- **Instant demat/remat** for above-ground flyover takeoffs and landings: new `TardisExteriorAction.DEMAT_INSTANT` / `REMAT_INSTANT`, `BaseTardisExteriorBlockEntity.dematInstant()` / `rematInstant()`, and an `instant` flag on `TardisSystemMaterialization` (persisted, set only by the flight system, so manual demat/remat from the console is untouched). Instant materialization takes 2 ticks.
- **Landing scan split.** `TardisSystemMaterialization` now separates a pure "look" (`findLandingSpot`, returns a `LandingSpot` with position and facing, changes nothing) from the code that applies the result. This runs on every real landing, so a normal flight is worth a check.
- **Key summon timer** uses `TardisSystemFlight.getEstimatedTripDuration()`, which knows about instant demat/remat, the lever-dependent flight time and the padding, and adds an estimate of the extra time fly-bys will add, judged from who is near the route at the moment the key is used. That is one pass over the players in the world when the key is used, and nothing while the flight runs. It is an estimate - people move - and was within a quarter of a second of the real flight in the cases I simulated.
- **`TardisSystemFlight`** keeps the flight state machine (countdown, waypoints, history) and hands the flyover work to two classes in `systems/flight/`: `TardisFlyoverPlanner` (stateless: which mode a trip gets, the flight time and caps, where it lands, who gets a fly-by) and `TardisFlyoverSession` (the entities and fly-by bookkeeping of the current flight, keeping the slam and the instant remat in agreement). The entities are cosmetic and never persisted: the entity refuses to be saved with a chunk, so a stale one can't survive an unload or a restart.
- **Flight progress on the console screen** uses the duration recorded when the flight started (synced with the flight system's data) instead of recalculating it. The screen renders on the client, which has no world to work the flyover's flight time out from; recalculating crashed it. The world getters on `TardisStateManager` now also return null instead of throwing when there is no world.
- **New lever** (`FLYOVER` control role): a boolean lever like `LIGHT`, same flags (only toggles while materialized) and same sound. It turns exactly like the light lever; its hotspot is `(-0.725, 0.605, -0.0875)`. That was cross-checked against the model geometry: the hotspots of the light, shields, doors, X/Y/Z and step levers all follow hotspot Z = 0.025 × the handle's position along the console.
- **Sound.** New `tardis_land_ground` sound event (`ModSounds.playTardisGroundLandingSound`) for the slam, playing `assets/dwm/sounds/tardis/land_ground.ogg`: a 3.4-second mono thud supplied by the author (mono, so it is positioned in 3D). The Dalek Mod's sounds are All Rights Reserved and were **not** used. A slam landing is also heard **inside** the TARDIS, at the console, as this same thud at full volume (unlike the other interior sounds, which are quieter than outside) instead of the long landing noise - and whether or not anyone is outside to see it: when nothing has planned the landing (no flyover was watching), the slam check at the moment of landing may load the destination chunk, which is about to be loaded anyway to place the exterior. Everything that only estimates (the key summon's timer, the flyovers' plans) still never loads a chunk to look.
- **Fades instead of the sky.** The flyover no longer shoots up out of sight before an underground landing or descends from the sky after an underground takeoff. It fades out over the spot before the normal remat, and fades in at flying height above the takeoff spot before flying off. The entity carries a synced alpha for this; under a shader pack, which ignores alpha, the model simply switches on or off halfway through the fade.
- **Water is open sky.** `WorldHelper.isOpenToSky` steps down through water above a spot, so a TARDIS can slam down into the sea (or a lake) and lift off from the bottom of it. Anything else that blocks motion, including ice, still counts as cover.
- **Manual demat/remat always uses the full fade.** An instant demat or remat is only ever asked for through `TardisSystemMaterialization.init(...)`, by a flight; `initDemat()`/`initRemat()` called directly (the exterior-shell change) clear the flag, and the console's control goes through `init` without it. So neither can pick up an instant one, whatever the flyover is doing.
- **The exterior shell is hidden in the view through its own door, with or without shaders.** Before, it was hidden in portal views only while a shader pack was on (`ImmersivePortalsIrisCompat`). Stepping back from the door inside the TARDIS puts the viewpoint behind the shell, so the police box showed in the doorway with shaders off too. `ImmersivePortalsShellCompat` now also hides it whenever the portal being rendered is that TARDIS's own entrance portal (matched on the TARDIS id); the shaders-on behaviour is unchanged. Not yet confirmed in-game.
- **Landing Shields lever** (`LANDING_SHIELDS`), next to the Flyover one: when on, the shields come up once the TARDIS has landed, together with whichever special shields (oxygen, fire proof, medical, mining, gravitation, special) were up when it took off. Takeoff notes them just before it drops every shield (`rememberSpecialShields`), and landing raises them (`raiseLandingShields`, from the flight's landing callback) with a single shields-on sound. It only applies to flights (a manual remat from the console leaves the shields alone), needs the shields system installed, and can be flipped in flight. New in English, Russian and Ukrainian (the last two unchecked by a native speaker).
- **Takeoff sound timed to the flyover.** An instant takeoff no longer plays the full 18-second takeoff sound over a flight that is much shorter. It is played by each client in earshot instead (new `TardisTakeoffSoundPacket` and `FadingSoundInstance`, because a sound the server plays can't be faded) and fades out inside over 1.5 seconds, ending 10 ticks before the flight ends; outside it follows the flyover (below), so the landing thud isn't buried. Normal (underground) takeoffs are unchanged.
- **A flyover keeps the chunk it is over loaded** (a `dwm:flyover` chunk ticket, radius 2, refreshed every tick after it moves, like an ender pearl). The entity lives in the world it took off in, and nothing kept that area loaded once players had left it, so a flyover with nobody nearby froze where it started while the flight itself carried on: the TARDIS landed with the slam effect but no flight, and the entity only moved when someone walked up to the takeoff area. This does load (and, in unexplored terrain, generate) the chunks along a route flight.
- **The takeoff sound follows the flyover through its lift-off.** Outside, an instant takeoff's sound is made by the flying TARDIS itself: it follows the entity and gets quieter as it rises, gone entirely by the time it reaches flying height (24 blocks up), and it ends with the lift-off (the whole ascent, when leaving for another dimension) with a 1-second fade, for every listener, so it can't ride along on a short hop, or a summon to the player, and play over the drop landing. Inside, it fades out before the flight ends, as before.
- **Fly-bys are heard.** A fly-by entity plays the in-flight loop for as long as it is there, following it across the sky at 0.6 volume, heard out to 40 blocks (it passes about 24 blocks up, so from about 30 blocks to the side of its path, loudest right overhead). The fall-off is done by the sound itself (`FlyoverSoundInstance`, a straight line from the listener's camera to nothing at the range), because the sound engine only fades mono sounds with distance and these are stereo. Both sounds are started by each nearby client, from a synced `SOUND` value on the entity (`TardisFlyoverEntity.Sound`, `FlyoverSounds`, `FlyoverSoundInstance`).
- **The slam's thud outside is played 5 ticks early** (`SLAM_THUD_LEAD`), by the flight system rather than the exterior. The sound file opens with about 0.2 seconds of near-silence, so fired when the exterior appeared it landed after the TARDIS was already down. The interior's thud is unchanged. It plays once, and the landing plays it if the flight never passed that moment.
- **In-flight loop** no longer starts a loop that would run past the end of the flight, so it can't play over the landing.
- **The "pop" when the light toggles is gone**, inside and out (`playTardisLightOnSound`/`OffSound` removed). The lever's own flip sound is unchanged.
- **Iris shader tail flicker.** Under a shader pack, the faintest part of a demat/remat (materialized value below 0.42, where the flicker starts dipping out of sight) isn't drawn, because shader packs ignore alpha and each flicker peak showed as a solid pop. See `IRIS_MIN_FADE_VALUE` in `BaseTardisExteriorBlockRenderer`; not yet confirmed in-game.
- **Languages:** the new lever and the slam subtitle are in English, Russian and Ukrainian. The Russian and Ukrainian ones were written without a native speaker, so they are worth a look.
- **Floating control labels** (the names shown over the console's controls when you look at them, `title.dwm.tardis.control.role.*.name`) are in Title Case in English: "Telepathic Interface", "Coordinate Scaling", "Outer Light", "Vertical Scanning Mode", and the rest. The X, Y and Z labels are exactly `X`, `Y` and `Z`, with no extra space.
- **Entity tracking range** for the flyover is now given in chunks, which is what the game expects: it was `128` (meaning 128 chunks, 2,048 blocks) with a comment that said blocks. It is now the render distance divided by 16 (16 chunks); the server limits it to its own view distance either way, so nothing visible changes.
- **`original-mod/`** (a local copy of the upstream mod) is in `.gitignore`.

### Known caveats / to verify in-game

- Not yet seen running: the heavy lift-off, the speed curves, fly-bys (they need a second player standing under the middle of a long flight to see) and the night lighting. Also worth watching: the handoffs at takeoff and landing (a possible flicker for 1 to 2 ticks where the flyover and the exterior overlap) and how the wind-down of the spin looks.
- Flights are longer than before when the lever is on, including small hops (at least 7.25 seconds; long hops scale with distance and have 5 seconds of padding, see above). The key summon's timer includes the padding and an estimate of the fly-by time, but it is only an estimate: players who move on or off the route after the key is used change it.
- The arrival flyover of a long hop and the cross-dimension drop only appear when a player is near the landing spot; without one, the landing is still instant, just unwatched.
- The Iris shader fix from 1.0.20 is not yet confirmed fixed in-game.
- Not done: an exterior monitor (seeing the terrain live from inside the console) was discussed and deferred.

## 1.0.20 — Flight, sound and block tuning

Commits `aa08a50` and `3baa7eb`.

- **Distance-based flight duration.** Flights take 64 to 900 ticks depending on Manhattan distance (reaching the cap at 10,000 blocks), plus 300 ticks when crossing dimensions, instead of one fixed loop.
- **Quieter interior sounds.** Takeoff, landing and the in-flight loop play at 0.6× volume for listeners inside the console room. The exterior sounds heard by onlookers are unchanged. Volume overloads were added to the `ModSounds` helpers; the old signatures still exist.
- **Rarer abandoned capsules.** Spawn rarity raised from 75 to 300 (about 4× less common).
- **Exterior light.** It turns back on automatically when the TARDIS finishes landing, and the lamp now follows the demat/remat flicker instead of snapping on and off.
- **Iris shader fix.** The exterior renderer no longer submits the shell's geometry when it should be invisible, because some shader packs drew an alpha-0 shell fully lit. Not yet confirmed fixed in-game.
- **Console unit recipes.** Netherite-tier crafting recipes for the Imperial and Toyota console units (Imperial: gold, diamond, quartz, netherite, amethyst, comparator. Toyota: iron, diamond, prismarine, netherite, redstone, comparator). Confirmed working in-game.
- **Block hardening.** Console units, engines and the Toyota spinner are unbreakable in survival and immune to explosions (creative can still break them).
- **Generated data is now tracked.** `common/src/main/generated` was gitignored, and a plain build ships a jar with no recipes, worldgen features, blockstates or loot tables, so it is tracked now and only its `.cache` folder is ignored.
- **Second console.** No code change was needed: the `consoleTiles` map already supports several working consoles per TARDIS. `getMainConsolePosition()` (used for some ambient sound origins) resolves to the most recently placed console.
